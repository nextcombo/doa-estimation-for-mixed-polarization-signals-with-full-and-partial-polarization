# DOA Estimation for Mixed Polarization Signals with Full and Partial Polarizations - Enhanced Separation of DOA [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Estimation of Direction of Arrival (DOA) for Electromagnetic Signals
The estimation of direction of arrival (DOA) for electromagnetic signals holds significant application value in military and civilian fields such as electronic warfare, radar, and wireless communications. One important characteristic of electromagnetic signals is polarization, which can be divided into two types: fully polarized and partially polarized [1]. In contrast to fully polarized signals, the polarization state of partially polarized signals is not fixed, which can be illustrated through the use of polarization-modulated communication technologies [2^,3^,4^]. When the signal exhibits a polarization direction different from that of a traditional single-polarization array, due to the mismatch in polarization directions, the performance of DOA estimation will be adversely affected. Polarization-sensitive arrays, which cover multi-polarization antennas, are particularly suitable for this problem [5^]. Many methods for DOA estimation using polarization-sensitive arrays have been proposed for both fully polarized (CP) signals [6^,7^,8^] and partially polarized (PP) signals [9^,10^,11^]. However, there are limited methods available for handling the mixed scenarios of CP and PP signals.
The ability to estimate DOA for CP and PP mixed signals is essential, which can be explained from two aspects. First, the coexistence of CP and PP signals is not uncommon. For example, in complex electromagnetic battlefield environments, electronic warfare receivers may intercept a mixed CP and PP signal. DOA estimation methods specifically designed for pure CP or PP signals are not optimal for this situation. Second, even when dealing with pure CP or PP signals, it is difficult to select an appropriate DOA estimation method without prior knowledge of the polarization state [12^]. Designing a practical DOA estimation algorithm for the mixed CP and PP signals presents some challenges. On one hand, due to the full rank of the covariance matrix of the PP signal, the dimensionality of the signal subspace does not equal the number of signals [13^]. Therefore, model order estimators such as minimum description length (MDL) and Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) cannot be directly applied to source enumeration. On the other hand, utilizing the feature differences between CP and PP signals is crucial for improving DOA estimation performance. Finally, avoiding (multi-dimensional) grid search can reduce computational burden.
The first study on DOA estimation for mixed CP and PP signals can be found in [14], where the authors introduce both the ESPRIT-based method and the MUSIC-based method. Subsequently, the ESPRIT-based method is further elaborated in [15]. The ESPRIT-based method involves introducing additional processing to the initial CP signal design method [16]. In this ESPRIT-based approach, the classification of CP and PP signals is based on the proximity of their eigenvalues, but this classification does not contribute to improving the accuracy of DOA estimation. The MUSIC-based method requires performing a 1-D grid search to compute the spatial spectrum. At each grid point, the eigenvalues need to be calculated. Wang et al. [17] proposed an estimator for CP suppression in PP signal DOA estimation, which then uses the same MUSIC-based method described in [14] to estimate the DOA of CP and PP signals. Finally, signal classification is performed using DOA association steps. Recently, literature [12] has improved upon the method described in [17], constructing a Sliding Projection Operator (OPO) after applying the CP suppression estimator from [17] to remove the PP component, and then applying rootMUSIC to estimate the DOA of CP signals on the mutual covariance matrix. Additionally, He et al. [12] also proposed a source enumeration method through candidate DOA selection criteria. However, these improved methods also have some drawbacks. First, the candidate DOA selection criterion requires a suitable threshold, which is difficult to obtain. Second, the CP suppression estimator suffers from CP signal leakage, not only negatively affecting the DOA estimation of PP signals but also damaging the source enumeration. Moreover, using the mutual covariance matrix for DOA estimation results in certain CP signals becoming invisible. Finally, due to the finite number of samples effect mentioned in [18] and [19], the constructed OPO is not accurate. When the power of PP signals far exceeds that of CP signals, the inaccurate OPO estimation causes severe PP signal leakage, leading to inaccurate DOA estimation of CP signals. Lan and Liu [4] proposed another CP suppression estimator for estimating the DOA of PP signals. However, this new CP suppression estimator still faces challenges with CP signal leakage.
This paper proposes a novel method for DOA estimation of CP and PP mixed signals. It first addresses the source enumeration issue. Instead of using thresholds, the algorithm infers the number of CP and PP signals by estimating the dimensionality of the two signal subspaces. On this basis, it utilizes the characteristic differences between CP and PP signals to propose an estimator that eliminates the DOA estimation of CP signals. This estimator can estimate the DOA of PP signals without the presence of CP signal leakage. Finally, by introducing the OPO (Optical Path Phase) algorithm to remove the PP component from the data, the DOA of CP signals is estimated. To improve the precision of the OPO algorithm, we propose two methods: one is an iterative algorithm that significantly enhances the accuracy of the algorithm; the other is an efficient, non-iterative approach that is effective even in high dynamic ranges of input signals. Since this method does not use the mutual covariance matrix, there is no problem with CP signals being invisible. Simulation comparisons demonstrate that this method has good performance and is compared with existing methods and Cramer-Rao bounds.
The article includes illustrations.
Conclusion: A new method for DOA estimation of CP and PP mixed signals is proposed. The practicality of this method considers the source counting issue and proposes a robust solution that does not use thresholds. Compared to previous methods, this method solves the issues of CP signal leakage and CP signal invisibility during DOA estimation. To enhance the separation between CP and PP signals, two new OPO estimators are proposed, one with high precision and another with high efficiency. Simulation results show that the performance of the proposed algorithm is superior.
